Introduction

  • Girish Karnad occupies a distinguished position in modern Indian drama.
  • His plays creatively engage with myth, history, folklore, ritual and cultural consciousness.
  • He reinterprets inherited narratives in relation to modern ethical, social, psychological and political dilemmas.
  • The study investigates ancient Indian ethical values in Karnad's English-translated plays.
Citation: Synopsis PDF, Page 2

Statement of the Problem

  • Existing scholarship focuses on myth, folklore, history, gender, politics and culture.
  • Ancient Indian ethical values have not been studied systematically.
  • Ethical concepts such as dharma, satya, ahimsa, karma and responsibility require focused investigation.

Research Gap

  1. Ancient Indian values not studied as a sustained ethical framework.
  2. Ethical dimensions remain underexplored.
  3. Ancient thought and modern dilemmas insufficiently theorized.
  4. English translations inadequately examined.
  5. Political ethics require deeper study.

Objectives of the Study

  1. Identify ancient Indian values.
  2. Examine ethical concepts in Karnad's dramatic vision.
  3. Explore myth, folklore, history and ritual.
  4. Interpret plays through Indian value systems.
  5. Connect ancient ethics with modern concerns.
  6. Assess significance of English translations.
